# Jurisdiction and Geographic Scope

## Purpose

This document defines the **initial jurisdictional scope** of the eutube project and explains the rationale for geographic limitations during early deployment.

These constraints are not ideological exclusions. They are **risk‑management and governance decisions** aligned with the project’s objectives.

---

## Initial Geographic Scope

During its initial phases, eutube is intended to operate **within European jurisdictions only**.

This includes:

* Member States of the European Union
* Jurisdictions aligned with EU digital, privacy, and data‑protection frameworks

Access from outside these jurisdictions may be technically limited or unavailable during early deployment.

---

## Rationale

The decision to limit initial operation to European jurisdictions is based on the following considerations:

### 1. Legal Coherence

Operating within a single, relatively harmonised legal environment reduces:

* conflicting regulatory obligations
* exposure to extraterritorial legal claims
* ambiguity around data protection and platform liability

This allows governance, compliance, and accountability mechanisms to mature before expansion.

---

### 2. Democratic Resilience

The project is explicitly designed to address:

* automated manipulation
* coordinated inauthentic behaviour
* large‑scale information operations

Restricting early deployment reduces exposure to **state‑level information warfare** and allows defensive systems to be validated under controlled conditions.

---

### 3. Infrastructure Integrity

Early‑stage platforms are structurally vulnerable.

Limiting geographic scope:

* reduces attack surface
* simplifies threat modelling
* enables focused iteration on bot resistance and AI transparency systems

This is standard practice for high‑risk digital infrastructure projects.

---

## Excluded Jurisdictions (Initial Phase)

During the initial phase, eutube does not intend to operate in jurisdictions where:

* extraterritorial platform control is asserted
* intelligence or surveillance obligations conflict with European privacy norms
* platform governance could be undermined by incompatible legal regimes

This includes, but is not limited to, **non‑European jurisdictions with expansive state access mandates**.

Geographic limitations are implemented at the platform level and are **subject to periodic review**.

---

## Review and Reassessment

Jurisdictional scope is not fixed permanently.

Expansion beyond Europe may be considered if and when:

* governance structures are proven
* transparency and audit mechanisms are robust
* legal and ethical compatibility can be ensured

Any change in scope will be documented publicly and justified against the project’s core principles.

---

## Status

This document reflects **current intent** at an early stage of the project.

It exists to ensure clarity, transparency, and accountability — not to foreclose future options.

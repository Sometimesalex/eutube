2. Threat Model (/governance/threat-model.md)
Scope

This threat model focuses on systemic risks, not individual content disputes.

The primary concern is structural manipulation, not moderation of opinion.

Threat Categories
A. Automated Manipulation

Bot networks simulating human engagement

Coordinated inauthentic behaviour

Engagement laundering via synthetic accounts

Mitigation direction:

Behavioural analysis

Rate and pattern anomaly detection

Human-verification layers designed for privacy preservation

B. State-Level Information Operations

Large-scale narrative injection

Cross-platform coordination

Use of synthetic media to distort public perception

Mitigation direction:

Jurisdictional scope limitation

Reduced attack surface during early deployment

Explicit AI-generated content labelling

C. Platform Capture

Economic incentives overriding public-interest goals

Algorithmic optimisation for engagement over accuracy

Governance drift under commercial pressure

Mitigation direction:

Non-advertising-driven design assumptions

Transparent governance documentation

Structural resistance to opaque ranking systems

D. Legal and Regulatory Conflict

Extraterritorial legal claims

Conflicting surveillance or data access mandates

Jurisdictional ambiguity

Mitigation direction:

Initial operation within coherent legal regimes

Clear jurisdictional documentation

Conservative expansion strategy

Non-Goals

Policing ideology or belief

Enforcing cultural consensus

Acting as an arbiter of absolute truth

The system aims to expose context and provenance, not impose conclusions.
